Method

Every radar image in this workstation is synthetic. A seeded procedural generator builds a 512 × 512 greyscale texture per pass from fractal value noise: an elliptical somma rim with sensor-facing slopes brightened and far slopes darkened, a smooth dark caldera floor, and a bright cone cluster near the Nakadake vent. A single fixed speckle field, roughly four-look, multiplies every pass so the grain stays identical between dates. Pixel values are display intensities on a 0–255 scale. They are not calibrated backscatter and are never labelled in dB.

Change between two passes is computed in the browser. Both images get a 3 × 3 box blur to suppress speckle, then each pixel is differenced (current minus baseline). Differences below 18 of 255 are transparent. Negative differences draw blue (the surface got darker, or in the seismic and tsunami channels, subsided or drew down), positive differences draw orange (brighter, uplifted, or crested). Alpha scales with the magnitude. Choosing the current pass as its own baseline yields an empty layer by construction.

The risk index on the observed passes and the probability on each forecast date are placeholders written into a small registry. The forecast values are not the output of a model: they are specimen numbers chosen to exercise the interface. Level thresholds (medium at 0.35, high at 0.60) are demonstration categories, not official ones. Risk extents on the map are schematic polygons seeded per date. They are not a hazard map.

Past the last sample pass the workstation changes state rather than inventing data. The last observed image is locked to 35% opacity as geographic context, the view modes and opacity control are disabled, the timeline thumb becomes a pale-sky diamond labelled FORECAST, extents and chart segments turn dashed, and a prediction card carries the disclaimer. No pixel brightness or point value is ever produced for a future date.

To replace the placeholders, point the configuration at your own imagery and timeline endpoint. When an external image or backend fails to load the workstation reports the error and hides the layer. It never substitutes a generated image for a missing real one.

The live channel

Channel 04 // LIVE NISAR reads the Earth Replay API. Events are areas tracked in its catalog; their scenes are real NISAR acquisitions found through ASF DAAC. Each pass date shows the scene's ASF browse image placed on its footprint. Browse images are quicklooks: real acquisitions, but not calibrated measurements. Where the processor has turned a scene into map tiles, those tiles are offered as layers. The live timeline holds only pass dates: it has no forecast half.

Two things on the live channel are modelled, and are always drawn hatched, dashed and pale sky with a MODELLED label: the event projection (a trend fitted to the event's own NISAR statistics, worded by an AI) and the earthquake outlook (the chance of at least one M4.5+ earthquake per grid cell, from the past year of the USGS catalog). Neither is an observation or a prediction of a specific event. AI briefings and answers are hypotheses and say so.

About

spaceapp is a prototype built for the NASA Space Apps challenge "Dancing with the SARs". It explores how a repeat-pass L-band SAR mission such as NISAR could be presented alongside a model forecast without letting the two be confused. The study point is Aso caldera in Kumamoto, Japan, at 32.884° N, 131.104° E, with two further channels (a seismic fault and a tsunami propagation view) sharing the same timeline to show that the forecast locks behave identically for every hazard.

The timeline carries six observed passes twelve days apart, ending at a demonstration "NOW" of 20 June 2026, followed by four forecast dates. The dates are design specimens, not the system clock, and NOW is not a claim about real satellite availability. Nothing here is real-time monitoring.

Limitations: all imagery, values, probabilities, drivers and extents are synthetic demonstration material. There is no NISAR observation in this build and no validated forecast. Seismic and tsunami units are illustrative. The basemap and fonts load from public CDNs, and when they are unavailable the workstation shows an explicit error rather than a substitute.

The live channel (04) shows real NISAR catalog events from the Earth Replay API, with ASF browse previews, AI briefings, a modelled projection, the earthquake outlook, area exploration and shared replay rooms. Sign-in is handled by Auth0. The three demonstration channels remain entirely synthetic.

Data credits: NISAR data courtesy of NASA/JPL-Caltech and ISRO, distributed by ASF DAAC. Earthquakes: USGS. Supplementary datasets: NASA Earthdata (GPM, SMAP, HLS, NASADEM, GRACE). The demonstration channels use no NISAR data. Basemap tiles by OpenFreeMap, map data from OpenStreetMap contributors.
